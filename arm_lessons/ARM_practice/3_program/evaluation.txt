Found it. reverse calls len, but it never saves lr.

asm
reverse:
    push    {r4, r5}        @ lr not saved
    ...
    bl      len             @ this overwrites lr
    ...
    pop     {r4, r5}
    bx      lr              @ branches to the middle of reverse

bl len puts the return-into-reverse address into lr, destroying the address
that pointed back into _start. When reverse finishes, bx lr jumps to just after
the bl len instruction instead of returning to the caller. It runs the second
half of reverse again, with garbage pointers this time, and eventually walks
off into unmapped memory.

This is exactly the thing I mentioned back on the first program:
the moment a function calls another function, it needs lr on the stack.
